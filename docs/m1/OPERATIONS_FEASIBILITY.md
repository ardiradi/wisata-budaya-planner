## Editorial capacity

The approved direction requires 30 distinct places total, including at least 10 food venues and at least 3 cultural-interest categories. This is a minimum launch inventory, not a claim that such an eligible inventory has been acquired. For capacity modeling assume 20 cultural places with two historical claims each, 10 food venues with one scoped dietary assertion each, one scoped accessibility assertion per place, and one current identity/location, hours and price bundle per place. Each additional tariff band, entrance, menu claim, language or accessibility property increases work. A single “accessible” tag cannot substitute for these scoped assertions.

The model uses the proposed maximum ages: identity/location 180 days, historical claims 365 days, hours 7 days, prices 30 days, dietary/accessibility 30 days. Intervals are upper limits; changes, expiry, corrections and applicable travel-date exceptions trigger earlier review. Retrieval is not human review. Preparation and approval are performed by distinct people. Minutes below are explicit planning assumptions, not measured staff productivity or market rates.

| Bundle | Count | Prepare / distinct reviewer minutes per bundle | Expected refreshes/week (`count × 7 / age_days`) | Combined hours/week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and main location | 30 | 12 / 8 | 1.167 | 0.389 |
| Historical claim | 40 | 15 / 10 | 0.767 | 0.320 |

| Price/mandatory-fee bundle | 30 | 10 / 6 | 7.000 | 1.867 |
| Scoped dietary/accessibility assertion | 40 | 12 / 8 | 9.333 | 3.111 |

Add daily queue/eligibility triage at 15 minutes × 7 = 1.75 hours/week; three correction cases/week at 20 preparation + 10 review minutes = 1.50 hours; six dated closure/event/exception bundles/week at 15 + 10 minutes = 2.50 hours. Total is **17.94 hours/week**, before a 25% interruption/absence reserve: **22.42 hours/week**. The refresh subtotal splits into about 7.46 contributor hours and 4.73 distinct-reviewer hours. With queue/correction/exception work and reserve, the split is **14.64 contributor hours and 7.78 reviewer hours/week**. These roles cannot be merged into self-approval to save capacity.

A holiday/event peak requiring all 30 venues' applicable exceptions rather than six adds `(30−6) × 25 / 60 = 10` hours before reserve: about **34.92 hours in that week including reserve**. Review before a known holiday/event, not after the ordinary seven-day queue. Review requests within the 90-day horizon need supported recurrence/effective dates and exception coverage for the requested date; a current weekly schedule alone does not settle a known special-date closure. Unknown applicability yields draft. Establish a daily duty roster, weekend/holiday backup and immediate unpublish path; do not promise all-day human response until coverage is named and funded.

For an initial audit, the same baseline bundles require 54.50 hours. Add 30 dwell-policy reviews at 8 + 5 minutes = 6.50 hours; 30 source-rights/attribution bundles at 8 + 7 = 7.50 hours; 36 independently sourced access points/approved anchors at 12 + 6 = 10.80 hours; and three scenario route audits at 60 + 45 = 5.25 hours. **Initial total: 84.55 hours; with 25% reserve: 105.69 hours.** This excludes travel, operator-contact waiting, difficult rights negotiations, credential verification fees and rework when evidence fails. ## Browser Maps spending exposure

An exposed browser key cannot be put behind the same transactional per-operation reservation as server routing. A page gate or lazy load reduces legitimate map events but is not a hard bound on replay or copied-key exposure. Keep separate browser/server keys and projects; use specific allowed referrers/API restrictions, supported App Check, smallest tested quotas and a rehearsed disable path. These controls mitigate abuse; no guarantee of an absolute USD 100 combined provider spend is demonstrated. [Google security guidance, “Restrict your API keys”, “Split client-side and server-side usage into separate projects”, and “Use App Check”](https://developers.google.com/maps/api-security-best-practices).

Google's Maps cost guidance distinguishes request quotas from billing; billing may lag up to 48 hours and quota/billing discrepancies exist. [Manage Maps costs, “Quotas” and “Budgets and alerts”](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/manage-costs). Current Cloud Billing also has spend-cap budgets, so “all Google budgets only alert” would be inaccurate. The eligible-service list checked today covers Gemini API, Agent Platform, Cloud Run and Cloud Run functions, **not Maps**; even eligible caps have noninstant enforcement and charge in-flight work/overage. [Spend-cap budgets, “Services that are eligible” and “Limitations”](https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/budgets-spend-caps).

Product Owner decision recorded on 2026-10-23: keep paid embedded browser Maps disabled pending separate rights and budget decisions. Enabling them requires an approved exposure envelope and actual SKU/quota/kill-switch evidence. An accessible itinerary list can link to Google Maps directions using independently sourced canonical locations; Maps URLs need no API key. This is an optional navigation handoff, **not a routing-feasibility fallback**, permission to copy Google output, or a privacy-free action. Disclose that opening it shares the URL's locations with an external provider; omit private anchors by default. [Maps URLs, “Introduction”](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/urls/get-started). Review the exact URL/display terms before integration.

Before enabling any browser maps, the separate proposal must establish applicable use/attribution/privacy rights and identify exact SKUs, account credits/tiers, units/day and burst quotas supported by those APIs, worst-case monthly paid units × unit rate including retry/error behavior, key restrictions, shared budget allocation, reporting lag margin, disable owner, expected disable propagation and acceptable overrun. Rights and budget decisions are both required. A daily request quota repeatedly reset across a month is not a monthly USD cap. Alert thresholds are secondary detection, not authorization to spend beyond the approved ceiling.
